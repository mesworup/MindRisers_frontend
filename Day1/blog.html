The government's contradictory policy: calls for investment on one hand, arrests on the other

The government has every right to reduce corruption and break the 'nexus' between politicians and businessmen, but the approach adopted in this process should also consider the negative impact it will have on an economy already struggling due to a lack of investment.

What should you know

  • The government has increased fear and legal uncertainty in the investment environment by arresting businessmen, bankers, and directors in the name of seeking accountability against corruption.
  • Detention during investigations, without distinguishing between business decisions and criminal intent, can affect credit flow, investment, and the pace of the economy.
  • At a time when Nepal needs capital and investment, the government should abandon the contradictory policy of calling for investment on the one hand and fearing imprisonment on the other.

Bhupu raised the issue of Gurkha recruitment, but the Indian Army Chief did not respond.

The recruitment of Nepalis into the Indian Army, which was halted in 2020 due to the COVID pandemic, has not yet been announced.

What you should know

  • Although former soldiers urged Indian Army Chief Dhiraj Seth to reopen the recruitment of Gurkhas, he did not give a concrete answer regarding the opening of recruitment under the Agneepath scheme.
  • During the visit to Nepal, the issue of Agneepath did not find a place even in high-level meetings, and the former soldiers have also demanded that the family pension process, which has been stalled due to mismatched documents, be simplified.

Pokhara — Former soldiers have demanded that Indian Army Chief Dhiraj Seth, who is on a four-day visit to Nepal, reopen the recruitment of Gurkhas. In a dialogue after a program organized at the Pension Distribution Office in Pokhara, Gaj Bahadur Thapa, central vice-president of the All Nepal Indian Ex-Servicemen Welfare Association and Gandaki Province in-charge, urged him to take the initiative to reopen the recruitment of Gurkhas. "We are raising our voice about Gorkha recruitment, you should also talk," he said, while other former soldiers applauded and expressed their support when he proposed this to the Indian Army Chief. "We have also put pressure here from the Gorkha Brigade to open regular recruitment. We are fighting, Nepal and India's relations are friendly, they will continue," he said.

The recruitment of Nepalis in the Indian Army had been stopped since 2020. At that time, recruitment was stopped due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and recruitment was not opened even in 2021. Later, on June 14, 2022, India introduced the Agneepath scheme for military recruitment, stating that there would be a four-year service period.
